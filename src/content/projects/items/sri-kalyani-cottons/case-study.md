## Context

Sri Kalyani Cottons is a family-owned cotton manufacturing and trading business. My role there — Operations & Trading Analyst — was my first real exposure to the fact that a business problem and a customer problem are usually the same problem viewed from two sides. This isn't a software project, and I won't dress it up as one; its value is where it sits in how I think.

## The problem

In cotton, quality isn't a footnote — it's the whole conversation. What a buyer needs is expressed in parameters like **staple length, micronaire, fiber strength, cleanliness (trash content), and moisture / contamination.** A customer asks "do you have what I need?" and the honest answer is rarely a clean yes or no, because their spec doesn't map neatly onto a single lot sitting in inventory. Separately, production had bottlenecks that were quietly capping how much the operation could put out.

## What I did

- **Worked directly with customers** to understand the quality and specifications they actually needed, and supported trade decisions against that understanding.
- **Analyzed the production workflow**, identified bottlenecks, and monitored operations to see where throughput was really being lost.
- **Contributed to process improvements** aimed at those bottlenecks.

## The useful idea: inventory as quality, not just quantity

The shift that stuck with me was thinking about inventory in terms of its **quality parameters** rather than just what's in stock. Organized that way, "we don't have that" becomes a much better conversation: the team could find a lot that genuinely matched a customer's need, or point to a **workable alternative** and discuss it, instead of hitting a dead end. That's a small reframe with an outsized effect — it turns a stock-check into a problem-solving conversation.

## Outcome

- Process changes I contributed to were **part of a roughly 25% increase in production throughput** — a team result I was one contributor to, not a number I'd claim on my own.
- Reframing inventory around quality helped convert "not available" into matches and alternatives for customers.

## What I learned

This is where I first learned the thing everything else on this site comes back to: **the best solution starts with understanding the customer and the operating system around the problem** — not with the tool you reach for. Years later, whether I'm building an internal platform, an analytics pipeline, or an AI product, I still start in the same place: with how the work actually happens and what the person on the other end actually needs.

> **Note on scope:** I've kept the cotton-quality detail at the level I can genuinely speak to — enough to work the trade and the operation, not specialist textile-science expertise.
