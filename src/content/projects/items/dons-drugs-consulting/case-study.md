# Don's Drugs — Finding $156K in Annual Revenue Opportunity for a Community Pharmacy

MBA consulting capstone exploring how Don's Drugs could turn existing patient relationships into sustainable growth through preventive care, medication adherence, and high-touch retention.

## Context
Don's Drugs is an independent community pharmacy in San Bernardino, California. The engagement ran through **MGT 238: Management Synthesis**, the MBA consulting capstone at the UC Riverside School of Business. Our three-person team worked with the pharmacy to assess the business, find opportunities for growth and operational improvement, and deliver actionable strategic recommendations.

## Problem
Don's Drugs had built valuable relationships with its patients, but several commercial and operational opportunities weren't being fully captured. Our analysis centered on one question:

> How can an independent community pharmacy increase the value it generates from existing patient relationships — while improving operational efficiency and protecting the high-touch service that differentiates it from the national chains?

The opportunities clustered around manual workflows that constrained capacity, refill recovery and medication adherence, underutilized preventive-care services, higher-margin pharmacy services, and patient retention. Rather than recommend aggressive expansion, we focused on **creating more value from the existing customer base and capabilities**.

## Approach
We combined qualitative business analysis with market research and financial reasoning.

**1 · Business & industry analysis.** We assessed Don's Drugs within the independent-pharmacy landscape — the competitive pressure from national chains, and the advantages smaller community pharmacies hold through personalized service and patient relationships.

**2 · Operational analysis.** We examined where manual processes limited the pharmacy's ability to consistently engage patients and capture revenue. A major focus was **refill recovery and medication adherence**, where better processes could improve patient outcomes, retention, and recurring prescription revenue at the same time.

**3 · Revenue & margin analysis.** We compared opportunities across services instead of treating every dollar of revenue equally, tracing the ladder from **traditional dispensing → medication management → vaccinations → specialty pharmacy** to find where the pharmacy could move into stronger-margin, deeper-relationship services.

**4 · Patient relationship strategy.** We reframed the existing patient base as a strategic asset — growing lifetime value through preventive care, adherence, refill engagement, vaccinations, and personalized service rather than leaning on new-patient acquisition.

**5 · Financial opportunity assessment.** We translated the recommendations into estimated business impact, identifying roughly **$156K in annual revenue opportunity** across the initiatives analyzed. This is an *estimated opportunity based on our analysis — not realized revenue.*

## Recommendations
The strategy centered on three pillars:

- **Preventive-care expansion** — use existing patient relationships to increase utilization of vaccinations and other preventive-care services.
- **Medication-adherence ecosystem** — a more systematic approach to refill recovery and medication management, reducing missed opportunities while strengthening recurring engagement.
- **High-touch patient retention** — lean into the core advantage of an independent pharmacy (personal relationships), differentiating on proactive communication, personalized service, convenience, and continuity of care rather than competing with chains on scale.

Together these represented an estimated **+$156K annual revenue opportunity** while reinforcing the pharmacy's community-focused positioning.

## Outcome
The engagement concluded with a consulting-style strategic presentation and recommendations delivered as part of the MBA capstone. Because this was a consulting engagement rather than an implementation project, the **$156K figure is an estimated annual opportunity, not realized impact.** The project showed how an established local business could use existing customer relationships, operational improvements, and service diversification to pursue growth without giving up the personalized experience that sets it apart.
