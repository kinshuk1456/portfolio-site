# What Backlog Readiness Actually Improves

Backlog readiness is often treated as a meeting cadence. In practice, it’s a quality bar for *what you ask engineering and QA to build and validate*.

## What it improves
### 1) Fewer mid-sprint surprises
Clear acceptance criteria and dependencies reduce last-minute scope shifts.

### 2) Better QA/UAT alignment
When stories include testable outcomes, QA can plan earlier and UAT has a clearer target.

### 3) Faster releases
A predictable backlog makes release readiness less chaotic: fewer reworks, clearer sign-offs, better communication.

## A simple readiness checklist
- The user pain point is clear
- The story is small enough to ship
- Acceptance criteria are specific and testable
- Edge cases are noted
- Dependencies are known
- Owner and stakeholders are aligned

## Closing
Backlog grooming isn’t about “more tickets.” It’s about making each ticket *more shippable*.
